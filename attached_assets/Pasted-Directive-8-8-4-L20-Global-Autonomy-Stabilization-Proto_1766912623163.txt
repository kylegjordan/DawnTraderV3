Objective

The Global Autonomy Stabilization Protocol (GASP) provides a supervisory feedback layer that ensures all adaptive subsystems — including the MOF, ARA, DCE, MACO, PDC-ECS, and APR-SLE — operate within stable dynamic limits.

GASP acts as an adaptive damping system to prevent over-correction, cross-module resonance, or feedback oscillation. It enforces long-term equilibrium between profitability, exposure, and systemic risk.

Core Goals

Detect systemic instability early (via variance in KPIs, control loop feedback latency, and cross-module coupling).

Apply corrective damping to adaptive weights, learning rates, and exposure multipliers when volatility exceeds thresholds.

Coordinate global pause-and-recovery states to safely rebalance subsystems after extreme drift or correlation spikes.

Preserve autonomy while guaranteeing safety — all subsystems continue learning, but within bounded ranges of adaptation.

1. Core Services

Create server/services/gasp-coordinator.ts

Aggregates live metrics from MOF, DCE, PDC-ECS, MACO, and APR-SLE.

Computes GSI, damping coefficients, and correlation matrix.

Publishes global state via in-memory broadcast.

Create server/utils/stabilization-controller.ts

Applies dynamic scaling to:

Learning rates (η)

Exposure multipliers (E)

ARA and MOF lambda weights

Provides rollback and recovery utilities.

Create server/services/equilibrium-restorer.ts

Handles auto-pause, cooldown, and re-engagement phases:

Cooldown if GSI < 0.65 for >10m.

Resume once GSI > 0.80 for 15 consecutive minutes.

Logs all events under /logs/gasp/.
