### A2 · Competing writes, ownership and compatibility

- `server/scripts/staging-liveness-watchdog.mjs:183–215` first waits up to 60 seconds for the normal CLI, then scans the file and directly appends after failure. The unlocked append can occur after a canonical reader has taken its snapshot and before it replaces the file. That loses the new row even though the append returned successfully. The failing CLI can also have committed before returning an error, so falling into the catch is insufficient to infer absence. The current fallback success line logs the key and leaves UUID, terminal state and delivery to be derived from the actual store.
- `server/services/system-alerts.ts:401–453` uses exclusive creation and records PID/time but permits contenders to unlink a lock older than 30 seconds based on mtime alone. The acquire retry budget is 50 × 100 ms. Sharing that existing algorithm leaves a live-but-old holder exposed, and compare-then-unlink without serialized reapers leaves an old-owner-to-new-owner race. The temporary dispatcher artifact has the same lock-file name and age-based takeover; check the source and installed hash again before any rollout. A mixed old/new writer is unsafe on this ledger.
- Installed Linux provides `/usr/bin/flock` (util-linux 2.39.3) and the ledger is on a local ext-family filesystem as reported by stat. Linux permits process birth identity checks with boot ID and process start ticks. Design fail-closed on inaccessible identity, including ambiguous or malformed locks. A bounded wait can safely defer the watchdog emission if the ledger is busy; retention and a guaranteed later retry need to exist independently of that ledger.

### A3 · Delivery and failed sends

At the audit ref, `scripts/system-alerts.ts:216–255` calls `fireDue`, which persists scheduled → active and `fired_at` under lock before the send. Both normal and #1191 r2 dispatchers use the same warning/critical delivery route. An HTTP rejection gives no success receipt, and a later ordinary due scan does not re-promote the active row. For an unresolved active critical alert, its first ordinary re-surface is due at `fired_at + 2h` (`server/services/system-alerts.ts:760–800`). For acknowledged critical the base is 4h. Once re-surface is due, failed delivery leaves the back-off unchanged and the existing timer can retry on the next tick. Only a successful re-surface advances delivery bookkeeping. The #1191 hold predicate is for its retained active warning IDs and VTS keys, so it cannot select the critical watchdog row. Initial `fired_at` is promotion evidence and the matched HTTP success is receipt evidence.

The normal watchdog healthy and no-longer-throwing branches call `resolveKey` for the open key (`staging-liveness-watchdog.mjs:415–430`). A newly replayed scheduled record can therefore resolve before promotion, eliminating both first-send and re-surface eligibility. Coltrane's isolated baseline produced zero receipts across both source variants on this ordering, versus two receipts across the same two due scheduled controls. He also reproduced 503 → 503 → 204 through each variant with one recorded re-surface. Separately, the generic add stamps creation before lock acquisition and a second resolution can change terminal timestamps (`system-alerts.ts:552–558,685–702`), so timestamps alone cannot identify an attempt.

## Implementation and proof plan

1. **P1 · Shared ownership and commit boundary (A1, A2).** Keep one plain-Node implementation, the canonical lock path and bounded asynchronous wait. Publish a complete unique token by exclusive same-directory hard link, with boot ID, PID and start ticks. Fail closed on ambiguous identity. After the age floor serialize reclaimers on a permanent bounded `flock` guard. Inside it, read the aged token and prove its process identity is dead, then read the canonical path again and require the identical owner token immediately before unlinking, with the guard still held. Abort on a changed/missing token. Normal release also acts on its own owner token. Once death is proven, that owner cannot release after the final comparison, and other reclaimers remain excluded through unlink. Normal acquisition can publish only when the canonical name is absent. Keep the reaper file stable and reject unverified legacy owners. Compare acquisition cost and prove both dead-owner schedules with the real Linux filesystem.
2. **P2 · Durable attempt, commit and healthy recovery (A1–A4).** Before spawning the primary CLI, atomically publish and flush an immutable attempt beside the ledger, outside the checkout. Its UUID is the proposed alert UUID and applies to the CLI and plain-Node retry. The per-key pending path allows one attempt at a time without overwriting an existing token. The narrowly tagged watchdog CLI path uses the same canonical lock and UUID, leaving ordinary CLI/add callers unchanged. Under lock both paths first check for that exact persisted UUID, including terminal, and otherwise require that the current pending token still equals their own before writing. A distinct unresolved same-key row can absorb the attempt under the existing dedupe rule; a distinct terminal row cannot, even if re-resolved during the attempt. A delayed CLI with no matching pending token cannot append a new row. Store a complete scheduled fallback row by atomic replacement of the original bytes plus one line, with unrelated bytes preserved. Flush the exact commit or matched ledger/directory before retiring pending. Recheck current pending identity immediately before retirement under the same lock. Pending remains intact after timeouts, process death or ambiguous persistence, with a named error when the attempt cannot be completed. Regular, weekly and failure-notify invocations retry published attempts before same-key bookkeeping.

   For a tracked warning/critical occurrence, the healthy watchdog path cannot resolve it until the ordinary dispatcher has recorded a successful receipt for that UUID. Initial success records a narrowly tagged receipt through the existing metadata writer after HTTP success; an existing successful re-surface record also proves receipt. Promotion alone does not qualify. Apply the check at the watchdog-authorized resolve under lock to the exact row, including the recovery-after-throw branch. When healthy comes first, record the existing watchdog resolution evidence in that row's metadata and leave the row unresolved. On the next watchdog invocation finalize any receipt-confirmed, previously observed recovery before checking for or minting a fresh occurrence. Update the watchdog's open-key view only for confirmed terminal results, including a separate deferred result in the CLI reply. Any repeat before the first occurrence closes remains subject to existing open-key dedupe. Scheduled retries and failed sends stay eligible for the ordinary dispatcher. An explicit operator resolution is terminal and must never be replayed. Routine info heartbeat uses its date key and remains outside the notification receipt guard. Report marker-write failure visibly and preserve the normal unresolved re-surface backstop; no exactly-once promise across an ambiguous external receipt followed by process death.
3. **P3 · Offline controls against real code (A1–A4; scope OBJ-1/2/3).** With the real watchdog, canonical writer and isolated ledger, force both read-to-rename orderings and require UUID and unrelated control retention. Hold a live owner past 30s, time out, restart, release and require the same UUID once. Race two reapers on a genuinely dead owner. Separately pause a reaper after its first read, let that owner release and exit, let a successor publish, then continue death proof and require successor retention; removing the final token check must fail. Kill after durable publication before/during the CLI and after a ledger commit before pending retirement. Cover delayed CLI under lock, CLI success, CLI commit then error, later terminal re-resolution of a different older UUID, same-UUID terminal readback, a fresh same-key attempt while the older worker finishes, healthy and subsequently bad runs, UTC weekly dedupe and `--self-fail`. Require exact UUID/durable state and reject both stale-worker appends and stale-worker cleanup of the successor attempt. The old unlocked append must fail the original hostile ordering.
4. **P4 · Receipt and re-arm proof (A3).** Use the normal and pinned #1191 r2 dispatcher sources as separate isolated baselines. Score the complete proof against the candidate normal dispatcher and the isolated #1191 source fixture carrying only the reviewed writer and receipt changes. Compare the latter's exact hold predicate and receipt path against the pinned baseline. For both forced write orderings, run the actual healthy watchdog after retry but before the due scan. The row must remain eligible. Match one successful initial receipt to the exact UUID in the body, dispatch log and durable metadata. After that receipt prove resolved old UUID and re-arm with a new UUID for a fresh occurrence. Repeat with failure 503 on initial delivery, ordinary intervening ticks before the 2h active-critical TTL, 503 on the first due re-surface then 204 on retry. Before success there are no success receipts and no advanced re-surface metadata. After 204 there is one recorded re-surface and the same recovery/re-arm behavior. For #1191, include a known held, otherwise due VTS warning as a positive control for the hold predicate. It stays deferred while the watchdog delivers. Keep all other rows and ordinary delivery schedules untouched.
5. **P5 · Activation and proof boundary (A1, A2, A4).** Require an owner-coordinated writer drain for both cutover and rollback. In the approved slot stop admissions to the app/API, dispatcher, watchdog (including weekly and OnFailure) and staging governance timers and failure hooks. Coordinate with OLD/INFRA for Helsinki drift cron and deploy-notice timer/gate, other scheduled producers from fresh census, and ordinary deploy/CLI/manual/maintenance calls. Drain or confirm termination of in-flight invocations and descendants, including SSH-launched CLI writers, and verify no holder or old writer can still enter. Pause and abort on an unknown writer or lock. Re-read exact unit commands, checkout, deployed build, bundles, clocks and binaries before either switch, then restart admissions only after they all use the compatible implementation. Keep pending files through rollback and replay on a compatible version, never through the legacy unlocked path. Activation waits for restored normal dispatcher or a separately approved compatible artifact. Preserve live #1191 containment and the joint recovery read. Plan Step 7 as isolated fault proof and immediate deployed identity/console/receipt-path readback in a registered, permitted slot. No elapsed soak or live alert injection is planned. If that slot cannot exercise the seam, carry verification pending. #994 Step 3 still waits for verified #647(i).

In plain language, the watchdog can currently write an alert just before another writer replaces the alert list, and the new row can disappear. The plan keeps a durable copy of that alert, gives its retry the same protection as the ordinary alert writers, and proves the exact alert reaches the normal notification route after contention and restart.
