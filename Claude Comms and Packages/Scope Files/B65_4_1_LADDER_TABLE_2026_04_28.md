## What each column means in plain English

| Column | What it is |
|---|---|
| Entry | Price the trade opened at |
| Orig Stop | The original stop-loss the strategy set at entry. Below entry. If price drops here, exit at a loss. |
| Orig Target | The original take-profit the strategy set at entry. Above entry. If price reaches here, the system was supposed to take profit. |
| Final Stop | The stop price as it stood at the moment of exit, AFTER the ladder ratcheted up however many times. |
| Exit Price | The actual price the trade closed at. This is the stop-trigger price MINUS slippage on execution. |
| Rungs | How many times the ladder ratcheted up. 0 = no ratchet (target never hit). 1 = one ratchet (target hit, then stop fired). 2 = two ratchets (target hit, then ratcheted target also hit, then stop fired). |
| Actual Net | The realized P&L percent on the trade, after fees and slippage. |
| Counterfactual @ Orig Target | What the P&L percent WOULD have been if we'd just taken the original target and exited there. Computed as `(target − entry)/entry × 100 − cost_pct`. |
| Ladder Δ | Actual minus counterfactual. Positive = ladder added profit beyond just-take-target. Negative = ladder cost us value vs just-take-target. |

## What the "anomaly" rows are actually telling us

I now think I understand the anomaly cases. The `target_lock_r` parameter (currently seeded at 1.5 in `module_constants`) means: **the ladder's "target latch" doesn't fire at the strategy's published target — it fires at "1.5 × R" of profit, where R is the entry-to-original-stop distance.** When the original stop is very tight relative to the target, +1.5R of profit is well below the strategy's target.

**Worked example using XMR/USD row 7:**
- Entry: $402.49, Original Stop: $388.02, Original Target: $431.44
- R (risk) = 402.49 − 388.02 = **$14.47**
- 1.5R = $21.71
- Target latch trigger price = entry + 1.5R = 402.49 + 21.71 = **$424.20**
- BUT — the original stop was very wide ($14.47 = 3.6% below entry), and the original target was $431.44 (+7.2% above entry, about 2R)
- Wait, that's 2R not 1.5R — let me redo

Actually for XMR the latch should fire near $424, but the actual stop is at $403.53. So target_lock_r isn't the explanation either. **Most likely:** the target_lock_r interaction is more nuanced (involves ATR-scaled distance or break-even latching first), and the data we have in the CSV doesn't expose the latch-trigger price.

**This is exactly why we need the CSV column additions** I proposed in the verification report — `latch_trigger_price`, `original_stop_price`, `rung_target_history`. Without them, the anomaly rows are unreadable. With them, we'd be able to say definitively: "for XMR/USD, latch triggered at price X, rung-1 floor was set at Y, rung-2 fired at Z, final exit at W."

## Why the multi-rung trades (rows 14-16) lost money

For RENDER/USD, RENDER/EUR, and TAO/EUR with `Rungs=2`, here's the mechanism:

1. Trade opens, target gets hit → rung 1 ratchet (target advances by +R, stop locks above original target)
2. Price keeps running up → rung 2 ratchet (target advances by another +R, stop locks above rung 1)
3. Price reverses HARD before reaching rung 3 target — drops past the rung 2 floor
4. Stop triggers, market sell executes — but slippage on illiquid pairs swallows the buffer
5. Final exit price ends up below where rung 2 floor was set, sometimes even at-or-near entry

For TAO/EUR specifically: target was 227.54, but the trade exited at 218.85 (just barely above the original entry of 218.41). That's a multi-rung trade that locked in essentially zero profit despite ratcheting twice. **The mechanism that's supposed to capture upside instead returned the trade to entry.**
