## 0. WHAT THIS BATCH IS FOR, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
Two geometry gates stand between a strategy's signal and a trade: the **reward-to-risk floor** (`expectancy_gates.min_rr`) and the **reachability ceiling** (`expectancy_gates.reach_atr_max`). ## 1.2 FINDINGS — each stated so it can be falsified

**F-1 — `strong_bull_trend` IS OFF ON BOTH CLASSES, AND ITS CEILING CANNOT BE FIXED BY A SMALL MOVE.**
Crypto 0 passes of 367,009 (98.65 % `unreachable`); xStock 0 of 406 (81.77 % `unreachable`). Its geometry is `stop = entry − atr × 3.0`, `target = entry + atr × 6.0` ⇒ **RR ≡ 2.0 exactly** and, because the guard divides by the **clamped** ATR, **`atrsToTarget` ≥ 6.0 always**.
⇒ **A ceiling of 5.0 admits NOTHING. At 6.0, `attGt6 / attGt4` says 40.49 % (91,490 of 225,978) still exceeds it on crypto and 47.71 % (52 of 109) on xStock** — those are the evaluations where the **10 %-of-price ATR clamp binds**, which is a volatility condition, not a target-distance one, and is set by a **hardcoded TypeScript constant, not a DB lever**.
⛔ **AND 6.0 EXACTLY IS A KNIFE EDGE BY CONSTRUCTION:** the guard refuses on `>` against a value that IS 6.0 to within rounding, while the second application divides by the **raw** ATR and gets exactly 6.0. ⇒ **the two applications would disagree systematically on 40 % of this strategy's signals**, and a ceiling set at the spike would make the gate a coin flip. **Any proposed ceiling is strictly above 6.0, and the batch states how far above and why.**
