## THE BOTTOM LINE (field-standard, and it REFINES my pre-research lean)
> **The EV comparison IS the right backbone — post passively iff `E[net edge | maker]` (fill-prob-weighted, adverse-selection-adjusted) > `E[net edge | taker]` (fill-certain). BUT a *purely* EV-driven rule is sound ONLY if its two soft terms are conditioned correctly: (1) the adverse-selection haircut must be conditioned on OUR OWN signal strength (a predictive signal adversely-selects its own passive fills); (2) the non-fill haircut must price ALPHA DECAY during the chase, not just the re-cross spread. Because those terms will be noisy — and we have NO live passive-fill history (VTS since Phase 8) — and a pure EV rule is most fragile exactly on the strong/urgent signals where mistakes cost the most edge, the robust field-standard design is: EV-as-backbone PLUS an explicit URGENCY/edge-strength GUARDRAIL that forces taker (or a short-time-budget make-then-take) when the edge is large AND the alpha half-life is short. Practical implementation = the MAKE-THEN-TAKE LADDER (post passively, escalate to taker after a per-class/regime time budget).**

## (1) Core framework — price improvement vs execution certainty
Taker = pay spread + taker fee, near-certain immediate fill. Maker = earn the spread + lower/rebated fee, but (a) may not fill, (b) when it fills it's disproportionately in the states where the trade was about to go against you (adverse selection). The make/take split is endogenous to spread, volatility, order-arrival. **[ESTABLISHED]** Foucault-Kadan-Kandel (2005, RFS); Harris *Trading and Exchanges* (2003); Cartea-Jaimungal-Penalva *Algorithmic and HF Trading* (2015). Decomposition: `E[post] = P(fill)·(adverse-sel − spread-captured − rebate) + P(no-fill)·(missed/chased-trade cost)`.

## (2) Adverse selection — the subtle critical part
A resting limit order is a **free option written to the market** (Glosten 1994; Copeland-Galai 1983; Glosten-Milgrom 1985): it executes when an informed counterparty wants the other side — your buy fills right before price drops. **★ CRUCIAL for us: adverse selection is LARGER exactly when YOUR signal is strong/informed — you ARE the informed trader.** Posting passively on a predictive entry means you only fill when the market hasn't yet moved your way → you systematically MISS the fills where your edge would have paid off and KEEP the ones where it didn't. **[SYNTHESIS]** A maker-EV that ignores the correlation between "did it fill" and "was the signal right" SYSTEMATICALLY OVERSTATES maker EV for a predictive strategy. Measured empirically as the signed **markout** (mid move at 1s/10s/1m/5m post-fill).

## (3) Urgency / alpha decay — Implementation Shortfall
The choice must be made against the opportunity cost of delay = Implementation Shortfall (Perold 1988): explicit fees + spread/impact + **delay/opportunity cost** (alpha that decays while you wait/fail to fill). Almgren-Chriss (2000) urgency parameter; Kissell (2013): the faster alpha decays, the more aggressive (taker-leaning). **[ESTABLISHED]** Standard rule: **match aggression to alpha half-life** — edge realized over minutes-hours tolerates maker; edge realized over seconds-minutes must be taken or it evaporates.

## (4) Fill probability
Modeled as f(distance-from-touch / queue-depth-ahead, order-flow-imbalance, short-horizon volatility, time-in-force). Queue-reactive (Huang-Lehalle-Rosenbaum 2015) or intensity form `λ(δ) ∝ e^(−κδ)` → closed-form `P(fill in T)`; practically a logistic/GBM fit on (depth, spread, OFI, vol, time) vs realized fill label. **[ESTABLISHED form; SYNTHESIS: fit per-class/regime from our own posted-order history once we have it.]**

## (5) Practical rules + the crypto fee gap
EV backbone (post passively iff maker-EV > taker-EV) + **match aggression to alpha half-life** + the **MAKE-THEN-TAKE LADDER** (post passively, escalate to a more aggressive price / convert to taker after a time budget) — this hybrid DOMINATES a pure binary choice (captures most of the maker fee advantage while BOUNDING the non-fill cost by the time limit). **[ESTABLISHED]** **Crypto fee gap [ESTABLISHED+SYNTHESIS]:** our July-9 Kraken Tier-1 = **0.80% taker / 0.40% maker = ~0.8% round-trip maker advantage** — enormous (vs ~0.1-0.16%/side in equities). That structural head start makes MAKING the strong default for any not-extremely-fast signal — which is *more* reason to nail adverse-selection + alpha-decay measurement, because the fee head start is exactly what tempts a naive EV rule to post passively on signals that should be taken.

## (6) Is pure-EV the right backbone? — yes, AS A BACKBONE, but it needs the guardrail
EV is the correct backbone (every rigorous treatment reduces to expected-cost comparison). A *purely* EV rule is sound ONLY if A (adverse-selection, signal-conditioned) and C (non-fill, alpha-decay-aware) are estimated correctly — and IF they are, urgency is ALREADY inside the EV (a fast/strong signal auto-produces large A + large C → EV picks taker itself, no override needed). **[SYNTHESIS] BUT** we won't estimate A,C well early (zero live passive-fill history), and a pure EV rule is most fragile on the high-stakes strong/urgent signals → the robust design adds an explicit urgency/edge-strength GUARDRAIL (a robustness bound against the two terms EV gets wrong, NOT a competing framework).
