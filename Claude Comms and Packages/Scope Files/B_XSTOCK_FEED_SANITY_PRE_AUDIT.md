## D. Plain-language summary
The audit read the real code, the live settings and the trade history before planning a line. It confirmed the exit check reads the xStock price from one place, on one timer, and that the one place cannot currently see whether the buyers' side of the book has vanished, because it throws that information away the moment it computes the price. So the first change is to keep those few extra numbers in memory. The second is the check itself: a small rule, with every threshold in the database, that says "this quote is real" or "this quote is hollow"; on hollow, the exit check waits and re-checks, but only for a bounded number of ticks, after which it decides anyway and says so. The audit also found that hollow books can reach a trade through two other doors — the emergency flatten and the manual close-all — so the same rule is consulted there, but it only labels; an emergency close is never delayed. Every trade gets a label saying what the book looked like when the decision was taken and when it filled; the money is never touched; the old trades are labelled from the archive where a quote exists close enough to the close, and left unknown otherwise. One learning store cannot be fixed by a label; it turns out nothing decides on it yet, but it is reset for this one class at deploy anyway, because the batch that will make it decide should not inherit a poisoned history, and the reset is written down. The most important thing the audit found is that our own price archive keeps only one quote every four seconds while the exit check sees every quote, so for nine of the eleven recent handoff closes the archive does not even hold the price the engine acted on, while it holds it for every one of the ten ordinary closes; that is why the guard reads the live quote in memory, why the old trades can only be labelled by the price that actually drove them or by the clock, and why the raw capture is extended to all three handoffs. Langston cleared this plan with three conditions, all folded: when the book is hollow the guard does not push that price into the shared price cache at all, so nothing downstream can mistake it for a real one; the settings the guard reads at boot are one list of twelve, and the start-up check refuses to run on any other count; and the historical labels that come from the clock alone are marked as such and can never relax the re-entry block — only a label measured from the actual quote can. Two things wait on Kyle: whether the entry side gets the same check now (his off-hours decision), and nothing deploys before 7 September. The exact thresholds and the wait cap are set from a measurement of the positions we actually held, which is running as this is written and is filled in before Langston sees this.
