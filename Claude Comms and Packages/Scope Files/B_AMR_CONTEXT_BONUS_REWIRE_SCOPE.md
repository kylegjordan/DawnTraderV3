## 2. ★ The load-bearing design decision — ANSWERED BY THE CODE, not by preference
The open question was: re-adding a context term to a scoring function whose weights were renormalised to sum to 1.0 — does the bonus come OUT of that 1.0 (re-split) or sit ON TOP (additive)? Getting this wrong silently re-compresses every score's range.
**The shadow module answers it at `amr-context-bonus-shadow.ts:206`:** *"WOULD-rank-1: live ranking + bonus (**the live composition stays untouched**)."*
⇒ **ADDITIVE. Return + friction keep summing to 1.0 and are NOT re-divided.** The bonus is applied on top with its own per-family `contextBonusMax` cap; the existing `Math.max(0, Math.min(1, score))` clamp bounds the result.
**Why this is not a preference:** the shadow arm accumulates evidence for the flip. If the restored live shape differs from the shape the shadow measured, the accumulated evidence answers a *different question* than the one the flip asks. The measurement defines the implementation, not the reverse.
**Two ceilings, do not conflate (correcting my own first read):** the `#221 ceilingSaturationRate` (`:64`, `:215-219`) measures the **net-return INPUT** saturating `NET_RETURN_CEILING` — the #221 leveling number — **NOT** the `[0,1]` score clamp.
