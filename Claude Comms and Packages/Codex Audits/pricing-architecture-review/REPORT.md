**Disposition: revise before making this document canonical.** The document identifies important problems, particularly loss of price provenance and an invalid feed comparison. However, several of its central explanations contradict executable code. Those errors propagate into its subscription, sizing, simulation and target-state recommendations.

The most consequential problem is not a demonstrated universal choice of the wrong feed. It is that a price can lose its meaning as it moves between feeds, caches, context computation, order simulation and accounting. Meanwhile, modeled execution sometimes assumes liquidity or a maker fill that the observations do not establish. Improving these contracts would make decisions and measured results more trustworthy. This review does **not** establish how much profit those changes would produce, or that pricing architecture alone explains the system's performance.

This report answers the brief's three requests separately. Code findings establish mechanisms and reachable branches, not their production frequency. Recommendations are labeled as such; no implementation or deployment is included. Existing risk limits remain hard boundaries.

## (b) My view of the best possible state

**Recommendation:** separate market observations, strategy features, order intent, execution estimates and accounting facts. Share those explicit contracts across lanes; do not force all five into a scalar called `price`.

The best source depends on the question. A valid BBO can describe the touch well. A checksum-valid L2 book adds size-dependent depth. Trade prints describe transactions that occurred. Bars describe an aggregation over an interval. None automatically establishes the price or probability of this system's next fill. More accurate execution measurement can lower apparent win rate or reported profit; that is a better foundation for improving real outcomes.

### The minimum data contract

Each observation should retain venue, asset class, canonical instrument identity, channel/producer, raw price kind, bid/ask and quantities where actually observed, and its snapshot/event identity. Keep venue event time, local receipt time, cache insertion time and decision time separate. For bars, also retain interval boundaries, latest constituent observation, coverage and forming/final state. Local receipt freshness does not prove a recent last trade; a recent bar start does not prove a complete bar.

For a book, add requested and acknowledged depth, synchronization state, precision metadata, checksum status and continuity epoch. Consumers should not silently promote unknown verification to verified. Keep ticker and book states separate, then expose a policy-selected observation with its selection reason. Do not turn a last-only fallback into invented bid and ask.

MCE caching must honor this contract: reuse only when the feature inputs and their interpretation are equivalent. An input/version-aware cache can preserve efficiency; a symbol-only context cache cannot guarantee the caller's intended basis. Smoothing should process identified observations with defined time semantics, not merely every repeated read.

These are proposed requirements, not claims that the fields already exist. They make uncertainty visible without treating every quiet symbol as broken or every recent receipt as fresh price discovery.

### Feed allocation and lifecycle

I would evaluate broad `bbo` ticker coverage as a practical default for current touch prices, with synchronized books for queued candidates, held positions and a stratified diagnostic sample. If measured throughput and decision benefit justify broad books, the design should allow them. Existing queue-time subscriptions are a starting point, not a feature to invent.

Use durable desired membership with reasons such as queued, held and diagnostic. Track desired, acknowledged and healthy sets separately and restore the desired union after reconnect. Releasing one reason must not remove another owner's subscription. A checksum resync still invalidates the local book and starts a new valid-data segment; pinning must not prevent integrity repair.

Kraken documents `bbo` as updates on changes to best bid/offer **price levels**, whereas `trades` is the default event trigger. This does not justify promising every size-only quote update. Verify request acceptance and the observed behavior; the documented ACK fields do not themselves echo every requested behavior. [Kraken ticker specification](https://docs.kraken.com/exchange/api-reference/spot-websocket-v2/ticker).

For paper taker fills, capture or replay the valid depth available at modeled order arrival, and label the assumptions about latency and liquidity persistence. Treat unobserved depth as uncertainty, not guaranteed completion at a convenient penalty. Emergency exit handling can prioritize liquidation while retaining residual position/exposure when completion is unknown. A simulator must not erase risk by declaring an unsupported full fill.

For maker fills, model the actual resting order lifecycle, including cancellation/expiry, partial fills and queue uncertainty. Until calibrated with execution evidence, publish model-sensitive results separately. Venue validation proves a narrower property—acceptability of a request—not maker fill probability or available liquidity.

### Implementation priority, if this review is accepted

1. Correct the canonical current-state account and its contradictory retained conclusions. Establish the pinned consumer and order-contract map.
2. Preserve observation identity/basis and fix context-cache equivalence. Separate independent feed states so subsequent experiments measure what they claim.
3. Address unsupported maker fills, synthetic exit completion and gate-to-fill snapshot age. Reconcile modeled costs with actual price movement to avoid double counting.
4. Verify subscription lifecycle and quote/book quality; then run the bounded comparison below. Use its evidence to select coverage, not the open-positions-only premise.
5. Version and compare strategy/trigger changes under unchanged risk limits. Establish explicit live-engine ownership and venue execution reconciliation before enabling live trading.

This order improves the validity of the next decision. It does not assume that adding more feeds, changing sides, or loosening admission will generate profitable edge.

### Replace the current three-arm ratification test

The idea of comparing `trades` ticker, `bbo` ticker and book is useful. Its present decision rule is not: it compares **cold bbo-versus-book error** with **hot trades-versus-book error**, changing both population and treatment. It can pass a poor bbo feed against a weak reference or fail an adequate feed against a near-zero reference. A finite test also cannot establish that a feed should be excluded permanently.

I recommend the following bounded experiment, preserving existing risk controls and existing evaluation cohorts:

1. **Pin independent streams and population before collection.** Use the same symbols and observation windows for all three arms. Retain separate raw producer states, per-symbol ACKs, channel configuration, precision and integrity state. Label the archived hot/cold criterion as an archive-activity proxy; throttled ticker snapshot counts do not directly measure true quote-update frequency. [server/services/passive-archive/ticker-batch-writer.ts:51](https://github.com/kylegjordan/DawnTraderV3/blob/9c2b50e7391758ef7fd2df0a48f0368fbcef13e0/server/services/passive-archive/ticker-batch-writer.ts#L51)
2. **Define eligible pairs by time and validity.** Record venue and receipt timestamps for each arm, alignment distance and decision time. Exclude or separately analyze checksum failures, incomplete snapshots, stale observations and resync gaps. After reconnect, start a fresh continuity segment only when the book is synchronized; do not carry a pre-gap observation across it.
3. **Compare within symbol and time stratum.** Evaluate trades-versus-book, bbo-versus-book and trades-versus-bbo on the same eligible slice. The book is a qualified reference, not an infallible ground truth. Measure sides as well as midpoint, depth-sensitive proceeds and which actual decisions differ.
4. **Measure tails and persistence, not just medians.** Report error distributions, adverse excursions, duration of stale/crossed conditions, event-to-consumption latency, partial-fill implications, CPU/backlog and data loss. Serially repeated reads are not independent observations; report distinct events and uncertainty using time/session blocks.
5. **Pre-register useful tolerances and a finite stop rule.** Derive tolerances from order size, strategy geometry and unchanged risk boundaries. Do not invent a universal basis-point threshold here. Set a maximum window and an insufficient-evidence outcome when coverage or precision remains inadequate.
6. **Investigate cross-arm crossings before assigning blame.** A crossing can arise from asynchronous valid observations, a bad ticker, a bad book or a resync problem. The current `>=`/`<=` definition also counts equality. One crossing alone cannot prove that book-based signal generation is the correct response “whatever it costs.”

A successful result would support a **conditional operating choice** for the tested populations, sessions and order sizes, with explicit monitoring and reconsideration conditions. A failed or incomplete result should name the failing observation contract and its practical consequence, not automatically mandate a broader feed.
